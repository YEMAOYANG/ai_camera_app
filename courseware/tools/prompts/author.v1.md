They are Python files that wrap an HTML body, a stylesheet and a script; you write those three parts as separate files.

You never stop early, never ask a question and never answer with an empty result. When a brief page asks for something that cannot be done honestly with these tools (checking stroke order, hearing pronunciation, verifying handwriting, anything that needs data you do not have), keep the page's goal and change the activity to one the page can really do: for example free tracing of a large light guide glyph on a canvas where the page only measures how much of the guide the child covered, while the teacher's voice names the strokes in order; or listening and picking. WHAT YOU WRITE (under courseware/lessons/<slug>/, new files only)

You may split, merge or add a page when the goals need it; keep the teaching order and cover every goal; say what you changed in your final reply.

In lesson.json declare each one as {"file": "assets/<key>.png"}. At most 8 pictures for slides and start pages plus at most 16 quiz pictures; reuse a picture for several scenes when the same place or cast appears.

THE LESSON IS FOR CHILDREN OF GRADE 1-2 WHO CANNOT READ INSTRUCTIONS
- Pictures carry the meaning and the teacher's voice carries every instruction. Cover all text except the character, pinyin or word that is being taught: the child must still understand the task, find the right pictures, operate and understand the feedback. Emoji alone, a decorative background or a speaker icon next to text do not count. The taught character, pinyin or word may be shown large; never rely on reading for anything else.
- One task per screen, big touch targets (the visible control may be about 60-90 px, always at least 44 x 44), recognisable pictures. The stage objects (what the child manipulates) take most of the screen; buttons stay small and few.
- Never show or colour the correct choice before the child answers. The first wrong attempt does not reveal the answer: give a spoken hint about what to look at, then a bigger hint, and only after the third miss show a demonstration. Wrong tries cost nothing.
- Everything in Simplified Chinese (简体中文). Traditional characters fail a release gate.

TEACHING AND ORIGINALITY
- Teach exactly what the notes say, to the depth they say. Our own characters, scenes, examples and exercises; never copy the official examples, story text, pictures or exercise items (a short factual phrase is fine). Keep one small cast and one picture style through the whole lesson so that the same character looks the same on every page.
- Every interactive page: the child's action changes objects on the stage (move, build, count, sort, trace, listen and pick, compare...), the result is visible, and a wrong try can be corrected. Pick the mechanism from the lesson's goals. Neighbouring pages with the same mechanism need a reason in the page's own variation (new knowledge decision, new condition). Do not make the whole lesson "tap the right card". A plain three-card choice is only for the quiz.
- Anything the child must count, compare or place exactly is drawn in the page by code (inline SVG or CSS shapes): AI pictures never carry an exact number of objects. AI pictures are for scenery, characters and single real things (an ear, a flower, a street) and never for numbers (the style text forbids text and numbers in pictures).
- What the page can verify and what it cannot: a page may check a tap, a drag, a count or a choice. It must never claim to check pronunciation, reading aloud, handwriting on paper or stroke order (unless the page itself draws the strokes from data in the notes). Reading aloud is led by the teacher's voice and the child's repetition is not scored. Do not draw stroke paths from memory.
- Facts must be right: Chinese pinyin with correct tone marks and the exact words of poems or sentences from the notes; numbers; science statements from the notes only; for safety topics only what the notes say.

PAGES (interactive)
- Page frame: use the kit. `K.hud({icon, title, levels})`, `K.owl()` (a caption bar at the bottom: `K.say(text, kind)` with kind '', 'ok' or 'no'), `K.ready({emoji, title, sub, btn})` (the start overlay: one big start button; the start picture is added by the builder from the scene's startImage), `K.levels(n, i)`, `K.star(n, fromElement)`, `K.confetti()`, `K.sfx.ok() / no() / pick() / tick(n) / win() / drop()`, `K.shake(el)`, `K.pop(el)`, `K.drag(el, {enabled, targets, onDrop, onTap})` for dragging with pointer events, `K.finish({title, sub, again})` for the finish overlay (a button to play again must reset the page completely), `K.el`, `K.$`, `K.$$`, `K.wait`, `K.shuffle`, `K.rnd`. The page script runs in an IIFE; never use alert, prompt, confirm, the network, eval, external fonts or images (pictures come only through the tokens __IMG:key__ and __STAGE__).
- Every interactive page: start overlay -> a few levels with a visible progress -> stars -> sounds -> graded hints -> finish overlay with 'play again'. The first miss never shows the answer. The page must be completable by a child with taps or drags only, with no dead end (a lost state must always be recoverable by a reset button or by the page's own logic). All objects stay inside the 1280 x 720 canvas and readable when the whole page is scaled down to 640 x 360 (no text below 22 px, buttons visible and at least 44 x 44).
- Teacher-led: the teacher's beats in lesson.json operate the page: at least three operations per interactive page ({"highlight": "#id"}, {"reveal": "#id"}, {"annotate": "#id", "content": "..."}, {"setState": {...}}) and at least three {"say": "..."} lines. The target of every operation must really exist in the page at that moment (an id in the html or an id the script creates, written in the form prefix-1, prefix-2... when created dynamically). The keys of a setState must be read by the page script in `K.onState = function(state){...}` and must move the page to a real state that the teacher is demonstrating (for example the objects the teacher counted). The teacher's first operation on the page can be the start (the kit starts the page when the target is not inside the start overlay); the teacher demonstrates one example, then the child does the rest.
- Teacher lines: simple spoken Mandarin addressed to the child, at most 25 characters per line (40 is the hard limit), one idea per line. Say what to look at and what to do; do not read the on-screen text aloud word by word; do not give the answer before the child tries. Each line is one beat; between lines put the operations that show what the teacher is talking about. Do not write lines in the name of classmates ("小满问：...") and do not use discuss beats yet.
- Slides: use only the six templates through scene data (lesson.json 'template' and 'data'), short texts (a title, a few short points), a picture from pictures.json. Teacher beats on a slide are {"say": ...}, {"spotlight": name} (name from the template: 'title', 'picture', 'caption', 'point-1', 'left-heading', 'word', 'pinyin', 'hint', 'step-1', 'item-1' ...) and {"laser": name}. A slide has at least two lines and one spotlight.
- Quiz: scenes of type 'quiz' with 2 questions each, exactly the format of build.py: every option has a label and a visual. Visual kinds: {"kind": "quantity", "count": n, "alt": "..."} for amounts; {"kind": "picture", "alt": "...", "picture": <glyph spec as in courseware/tools/glyph_picture.py>} for simple drawn pictures built from emoji (a number of identical objects, an arrow, a shape); {"kind": "asset", "image": "<key of an images entry>", "alt": "..."} for a real thing that an emoji arrangement cannot show unmistakably (a body part, an animal, a place, a tool): declare the picture in pictures.json (aspect "1:1", one subject, plain background) and as an entry of lesson.json images, then use its key. A question may also have a `visual` (the same kinds) that shows the situation. The picture of every option must be recognisable as exactly the thing the option means, and the options of one question must be clearly different from each other; an independent checker will look at every picture and compare it with the question. Options are pictures, not words (labels are short, the child hears the question read by the teacher); one right answer; `skill` is the brief's knowledge point; `analysis` explains in one sentence; use new numbers, words and situations (never those of the lesson pages); spread the correct answers over A, B and C (not more than half of all questions on one letter).
- No `discuss` beats in this version.

VISUAL QUALITY (an owner and an independent checker will look at screenshots of your pages)
- The things the child works with on an interactive page are drawn as inline SVG (or CSS shapes) at least 120 px big, with clear shapes and colours from the kit's CSS variables, or they are a lesson picture shown through __IMG:key__. An emoji is never the main object of a page (it is a small decoration at most, it differs from system to system and often does not show what you mean). Do not put a row of emoji where a drawing is needed.
- Controls for grade 1-2 are pictures or icons, not words. The only words on a page are the character, pinyin or word being taught. No label buttons such as "check" or "next" in text; use a round icon button (a tick, an arrow, a speaker) of 60-90 px.
- Each interactive page has 5-8 teacher lines and 4-6 teacher operations: three is the floor of the gate, not the target. The teacher shows, asks, points and demonstrates; the child then does the same.
- The mechanism must be the knowledge itself, not a reward gesture: for example "drag each sound card to the sense organ that hears it", "put counters one by one into the ten-frame and watch the number change", "trace the big guide character", never "drag a star into a box" or "tap the glowing thing". Ask yourself what the child's hands do that a child who does not know the lesson could not do.
- Writing or tracing pages: a big (at least 400 px) light guide of the taught character on a canvas; the child traces it with a finger; the page measures only how much of the guide has been covered and says nothing about stroke order or shape. Stroke order is told by the teacher's voice.
- If screenshots of the pilot lesson are attached to your task, look at them first: they show the bar for size, colour, density and finish.
